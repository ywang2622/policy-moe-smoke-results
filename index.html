The honest result

PolicyMoE did not beat the simpler systems on this small test. The pipeline worked, the experts trained, and the router learned a meaningful pattern—but the frozen model remained strongest on Levels 1 and 2. That is a useful result, not a failure to hide.

Sample questions

What the three levels look like.

Each item is tied to an exact passage in a source document. These examples come from the held-out set, not the training set.

Level 1 · MemorizationMultiple choice

In the May 28, 2024 announcement on voluntary carbon markets, what quantity is one carbon credit intended to represent?

  1. One kilogram of CO₂ equivalent
  2. Ten tonnes of CO₂ equivalent
  3. One hundred tonnes of CO₂ equivalent
  4. One tonne of CO₂ equivalent reduced or removed beyond what would otherwise occur

Answer: D. This checks direct recall of the document’s terminology.

Level 2 · UnderstandingMultiple choice

Which interpretation of the 30% investment-credit provision is supported by the March 29, 2024 Section 48C announcement?

  1. Labor compliance replaces project certification.
  2. Support for certified investment is linked to prevailing-wage and apprenticeship requirements.
  3. Certification makes the labor requirements irrelevant.
  4. Projects may choose either wage compliance or apprenticeship compliance.

Answer: B. This asks the reader to understand how two conditions relate, not simply repeat a number.

Level 3 · ApplicationOpen response

An EECBG-eligible town wants equipment rebates with less administration, but it is also considering a traditional grant with in-person implementation support. Identify both application routes, explain the voucher’s purpose, connect the Community Energy Fellow to the correct route, and give the two deadlines.

A strong answer should say: choose between a traditional grant and a voucher; the voucher covers equipment rebates or technical assistance with less administrative burden; Fellow support is associated with traditional grants; the local-government application deadline was April 30, 2024, while the Fellow-support deadline was March 5, 2024.

How it worked

A plain-English version of the pipeline.

The design is inspired by PolicyLLM’s three cognitive levels and PolicyMoE architecture. This is a small independent pilot, not a reproduction of the paper’s 21,000-case benchmark.

  1. 01

    Read the policies

    Start with raw public policy documents and keep the exact supporting passage for every question.

  2. 02

    Write three kinds of questions

    Facts, interpretation, and real-world application—matching the paper’s cognitive levels.

  3. 03

    Hold out whole documents

    A model never sees training questions from the same source as a test question.

  4. 04

    Train four adapters

    One general LoRA and three specialists: memory, understanding, and application.

  5. 05

    Learn the routing

    A linear router chooses one specialist from the question alone, then every system answers the same test.

Read this before quoting the numbers

This is a pilot, not a leaderboard.

  • Only five documents were held out for testing.
  • The differences are not statistically significant.
  • Seven open-ended answers still need two independent judges.
  • The questions were authored and editorially reviewed; they are not the paper’s benchmark questions.
  • The run tests whether the pipeline works. It does not establish that PolicyMoE is better.
